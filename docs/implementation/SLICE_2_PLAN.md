# Slice 2 Plan — Persistence + Identity/RBAC Foundation

**Status:** Slice 2 approved for implementation; this technical-contract update is documentation only and does not itself create a schema, migration, provider, route, dependency, or UI. The source of scope is item 2, **“Persistence + identity/RBAC foundation,”** in the [Phase 2 vertical-slice roadmap](../ROADMAP_PHASE_2.md), not a generic interpretation of “Slice 2.” The [Phase 1 decision register](../requirements/OPEN_PRODUCT_DECISIONS.md) and [permission matrix](../requirements/ROLE_PERMISSION_MATRIX.md) remain authoritative for product behavior.

## 1. Objective and exact roadmap scope

Establish the first real PostgreSQL/Prisma persistence boundary, verified account and guest ownership proof, event-scoped assignment/authorization, and append-oriented audit evidence on top of Slice 1. The roadmap names **reviewed Prisma migrations for event/identity/assignment/audit core**, **account/guest proof**, and **central action/resource authorization**. Its acceptance calls for a disposable PostgreSQL migration, cross-event/owner/gate denial, OTP abuse/replay tests, and audited grants. Its prerequisite is Slice 1 **plus session/OTP and identity/locking decisions**. The approved human decisions in §12 now settle those architectural/product/security choices; remaining implementation parameters are identified without reopening them.

This is the foundation for Slice 3 event lifecycle/discovery, Slice 4 registration/QR, Slice 5 gate attendance, and Slice 11 volunteer tasks/restricted audit search. It is not yet a full participant-to-event business journey. The numbered [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) Phase 4 describes the same database/auth/RBAC grouping; the vertical-slice roadmap determines implementation order after Phase 2.

## 2. Included functionality and hard exclusions

**Included, once gates below are resolved:** a disposable local/CI PostgreSQL instance; a reviewed initial migration for only the identity, persisted Session, event-scope, staff-assignment, minimal gate-scope, and audit facts actually needed for authorization tests; one Prisma data-access layer; an account proof and bounded guest email/phone OTP ownership boundary; a central server-side actor/action/event/resource policy; atomic, audited role grants/revocations; safe auth/authorization errors and database readiness; synthetic integration/negative tests. Add configuration, scripts, and dependencies only when used by these capabilities.

**Excluded:** event creation/configuration/publication/Live/completion/cancellation UI or product API (Slice 3); PUBLIC catalog, PRIVATE controlled-link mechanics, registration availability, and registration records (Slices 3–4); QR credentials (4); full gate configuration/scanning, `scan_id`, attendance, occupancy/corrections (5); live transport, alerts, forecasts, certificates, delivery, results, and volunteer **task** records (6–11). Do not introduce Kafka, microservices, event sourcing, a second ORM, a generic repository/service framework, production infrastructure, or speculative future tables. A minimal gate identity may exist solely to make assigned-gate scope a database fact; it is not a working scanner or gate-management feature. No Phase 1 screen is claimed complete.

Preserve the locked rules as **authorization boundaries**, not prematurely implemented workflows: Organizer owns an event and alone controls lifecycle/Admin grants; Admin is assigned-event only; Gate/Security is assigned-gate only with no contact data; Volunteer has no general access; Participant/Guest owns only verified self records. Event state is not registration availability, the one capacity is a REGISTERED-registration cap, and only accepted attendance transitions affect occupancy. Those later-slice invariants need no tables or commands here.

## 6. Authentication/identity and RBAC boundary

The application needs a trustworthy verified account subject, a **15-minute signed JWT** account session, an internal actor ID/type and current server-side authorization facts. Accounts are provisioned only by controlled seed/administrative process in Slice 2, with an associated unique normalized verified email and/or phone. **An account JWT may be issued only after successful OTP verification of a contact already associated with that account.** Supplying a contact, finding an account, generating an OTP or attempting delivery is never sufficient. Persist a Session associated with that `User` and include its unique identifier as `sid` in the JWT. The JWT has only required `sub`/user ID, `sid`, `iss`, `aud`, `iat`, `exp`, and `jti` if independently needed. Signing material stays server-side and never enters Vite/browser variables; practical asymmetric signing is permitted, otherwise a strong server-side HMAC secret is acceptable for MVP. JWT expiry is 15 minutes, but the JWT alone is not revocation authority: **every authenticated request** verifies signature and JWT expiry, then confirms the persisted `sid` exists, belongs to `sub`, has not been revoked and has not expired. A missing, mismatched, expired, revoked or unreadable Session denies access, even before JWT expiry. Do not store the raw JWT or build a JWT blacklist. Serve the JWT only in an **HttpOnly session cookie**, never localStorage, sessionStorage or browser-readable app state. Production cookies require `Secure` and an appropriate `SameSite` setting; local development may differ without weakening production. Cookie-authenticated writes require reviewed CSRF protection—JWT signing does not remove CSRF risk. Normal `POST /auth/logout` authenticates the current Session, persists revocation of **that Session only**, then clears the cookie. Other active Sessions of the same User remain valid; account-wide/logout-all is not a Slice 2 capability. Cookie clearing alone never substitutes for persisted revocation on the next request.

Guest proof is a **separate 15-minute signed token** bound to the normalized OTP-verified email or phone, its proof purpose and applicable event/context. It is not an account session, cannot issue an account JWT or grant Organizer/Admin/Gate privileges, and cannot become a permanent account credential. The Slice 2 roadmap requires account/guest proof; keep the proof boundary minimal and do not build a guest registration/recovery product workflow. The application generates a **six-numeric-digit OTP**, stores only a cryptographic hash, expires it after **5 minutes**, permits at most **5 verification attempts**, enforces a **60-second resend cooldown**, and accepts only the latest active OTP for a normalized contact/channel. Successful verification immediately invalidates it; expired, used and locked OTP cannot be reused. Delivery uses a replaceable sender: **SMTP for email** and an **Android gateway for SMS in the MVP demonstration**. Auth/business logic must not depend on either sender implementation. Email normalization trims surrounding whitespace and lowercases; phone uses E.164-style representation; no provider-specific transformations are assumed. Contact storage/protection, account recovery, exact cookie `SameSite`/CSRF mechanism, signing-key rotation and Android-gateway protocol still need technical review. No client-supplied role, user ID, verified flag, event ID or OTP-success flag is authority. A real API without configured proof fails closed.

Development/test mode may use a deterministic sender/test-retrieval mechanism with synthetic identities **only when explicitly enabled**; it must never be silently enabled in production. There is no public OTP bypass, test-header login, production secret or real recipient. Tests may inject a verified actor through an **in-process, test-only** seam; that proves policy behavior, **not** OTP abuse/replay. Roadmap acceptance still requires tests of six-digit generation, hash-only storage, five-minute expiry, five-attempt limit, 60-second cooldown, latest-only issue, consume/replay/lockout rejection, unknown-contact privacy and required audit/security-control failure. A stub that always succeeds does not satisfy Slice 2. Test retrieval may expose a code only inside the explicitly enabled isolated test facility; production diagnostics/logs must never expose plaintext OTP.

Central authorization takes `{actor, action, event, resource/scope}` and denies by default. It verifies ownership and current **independently persisted** assignment from server-side facts, scopes reads in the database query, and rechecks grants/owner under a transaction for privileged writes. Organizer alone may grant/revoke Event Admin; Organizer or an assigned Event Admin may assign permitted gate staff and Volunteer event scope under the locked matrix. Event Admin cannot promote another Admin or Organizer, cancel an event, transfer ownership, or gain manual registration-closure authority. A user may hold multiple legitimate assignments when each is separately granted; identical duplicate grants are constrained by the database, revoking one does not remove unrelated grants, and users cannot self-elevate. Gate/Security is limited to its assigned gate. A Volunteer role grant creates no task or general operational permission; own-task access waits for its bounded Slice 11 workflow. Participant/guest own-record actions are only a policy boundary here—no registration exists yet. Assignment revocation affects the next request because policy reads current assignments; account-session revocation affects the next request because authentication reads the current persisted Session. Live-channel invalidation is tested when live transport exists.

## 7. API, frontend, and request/data flow

The [API contract](../api/API_CONTRACT.md) names **representative resources, not final URLs**. Slice 2 needs the narrow application-generated OTP challenge/verify boundary with replaceable delivery, account cookie/JWT issuance **only after successful verification of an account-associated channel and Session persistence**, guest proof issuance only after successful guest-channel verification, authenticated `POST /auth/logout` that revokes the current persisted Session and clears its cookie, a protected current-actor/verified-scope read, and a scoped grant/revoke/read assignment command/query sufficient to prove audited grants. Do not invent a refresh-token, JWT blacklist, or logout-all service: the approved account JWT expires after 15 minutes and each protected request checks its persisted Session. Exact paths other than the approved logout action, payloads, idempotency keys, guest-proof transport, CSRF mechanism and whether grants are exposed as an HTTP route now versus first exercised through an application service against fixture events require contract review before coding; do not present this list as finalized OpenAPI. No event create/transition, discovery, registration, scan, volunteer task, or audit-search product endpoint is included. A safe operational database readiness check may be added (`/health/ready` or equivalent) because Slice 2 introduces a required dependency; `/health/live` must remain unchanged and not reveal connection details.

No Phase 1 product screen is required by the exact Slice 2 roadmap. The frontend may need only a small, accessible account/guest proof entry and self-session state **if** browser participation is needed to prove the approved mechanism; do not create event/registration/gate/admin screens or pretend the Slice 1 diagnostic is a product shell. The API/database integration and negative authorization tests are the primary Slice 2 evidence. Any browser proof UI must handle pending, invalid/expired proof, rate limited, session expired, forbidden and unknown/network outcome without exposing contacts or internal details; final navigation waits for a reviewed UX/API contract.

**Account path:** controlled provisioned `User` plus unique normalized verified email/phone → request verification → generate/hash six-digit OTP → deliver through replaceable sender, replacing any prior active OTP for that contact/channel subject to 60-second cooldown → submit OTP → verify hash, five-minute expiry, five-attempt/security-control state and association with that account → immediately consume OTP → **only then** persist a 15-minute Session and issue a signed JWT carrying its `sid` in an HttpOnly cookie → on every authenticated request validate JWT signature/expiry and current Session existence/owner/revocation/expiry before linking internal `User`/current actor (including platform Organizer capability where relevant) → scoped policy → query or short transaction → safe response plus correlation ID. **Logout path:** authenticate current `sid` → durably mark only that Session revoked → clear the cookie; other User Sessions stay valid, while replay of the revoked JWT fails on the next request. **Guest path:** generate/hash OTP for a normalized email/phone → send through replaceable sender → atomically verify bounded challenge state → issue separate 15-minute signed proof for that contact, purpose and applicable event/context → no staff privilege or permanent guest account; registration use waits for Slice 4. **Grant path:** authenticated actor → current event owner/authorized assignment check → target verified account and gate/event consistency → within one `READ COMMITTED` transaction check current assignment, mutate it and append sanitized required audit → return effective scope; concurrent identical grant loses to database uniqueness with safe conflict/already-assigned result. Organizer alone grants Event Admin; assigned Admin may perform only the gate/volunteer assignments allowed by the matrix. Cross-event, stale grant, self-escalation and duplicate requests must not grant access. If PostgreSQL is down, protected requests that cannot check Session state, protected writes and OTP verification requiring persisted security-control state fail safely and readiness is unavailable while process liveness can remain alive.

## 8. Validation, transactions, errors, and security

Validate bounded request shapes, IDs, explicitly typed contact and normalization (trim/lowercase email; E.164-style phone), proof signature/issuer/audience/expiry/purpose/replay as applicable, six-digit OTP shape, five-minute expiry, five-attempt budget and 60-second resend cooldown, target account-associated verified contact, event/gate relation, role/action legitimacy and current assignment state. For an account JWT, require `sid` and verify the matching persisted Session's subject, revocation and expiry on every authenticated request; a JWT with a valid signature alone never establishes an active session. A known ID never implies access. Use privacy-safe responses for unknown contacts and private resources; do not log raw contact, OTP, account JWT, guest proof, signing secret, proof payload or request body. Account JWTs travel only in HttpOnly cookies with production `Secure` and an appropriate `SameSite` value; CSRF protection is required for cookie-authenticated writes. CORS, exact cookie/CSRF/origin handling and rate limiting must be reviewed with the real POST routes; Slice 1's GET-only CORS setting is **not** sufficient for Slice 2.

Grant/revoke and the corresponding **critical** audit record commit together or fail together in one **short `READ COMMITTED` PostgreSQL transaction**: verify current trusted event owner/authorized assignment, target/event/gate scope and current grant, mutate the independent assignment, append required durable audit, then commit. Organizer authority is mandatory for Event Admin grants/revokes; the locked matrix also permits an assigned Admin to assign Gate/Security or Volunteer scope, not to promote an Admin or Organizer. Do **not** take a broad Event row lock merely for staff grants. A database uniqueness constraint is the final authority on concurrent identical effective grants; the losing operation returns a safe conflict/already-assigned result. Concurrent revoke/grant and stale authority must still be tested against current scope, not assumed safe from Prisma query order. Organizer-capability bootstrap/grant/removal is likewise a privileged state change requiring atomic durable audit. OTP attempt counts, cooldowns and lockouts are security-control state: if they cannot be persisted, reject verification; never accept based only on a valid hash. Do not hold a database transaction while delivering OTP through SMTP/Android gateway. Security-relevant events covered by [FR-AUD-001](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS.md), including privileged denial attempts required by the [auth/RBAC architecture](../security/AUTH_RBAC_ARCHITECTURE.md), require **durable audit events**. A required audit-write failure cannot be papered over with logs: maintain the access denial and return a safe failure rather than claim an auditable denial completed. Diagnostic logs/telemetry about failed authentication or permission denials may be **best-effort duplicates** of durable evidence; their failure does not weaken required audit. Where an event is only diagnostic telemetry and no existing requirement calls for durable audit, safe logging may be best-effort. Neither surface contains OTP codes, tokens, passwords/secrets, unnecessary contact or sensitive credential material. Event owner immutability, relationship FKs and unique effective grants are database-enforced. There is no registration-capacity or scan concurrency transaction in this slice. Later registration/scan operations have their own separately approved rules.

Use the common safe `{code, message, correlation_id, details?, retryable?}` envelope for new protected API errors: distinguish 401 missing/expired proof, 403 unauthorized scope, privacy-safe 404, 409 stale/duplicate grant, 429 OTP abuse throttle and 503 required dependency unavailable where applicable. Exact code catalog and HTTP paths need contract approval. Client timeout/ambiguous delivery outcome is **unknown**, not evidence of a completed grant or verified guest. No stack trace, SQL, sender diagnostics, PII or secret appears in API responses, audit metadata or logs. Keep application database credentials least-privileged; protect personal data in transport, storage, backups and fixtures. Data retention/deletion policy remains a pre-real-data gate, not an invented duration.

## 12. Seven decision-resolution records

Statuses incorporate the approved human decisions above. **RESOLVED** means the architectural/product decision is settled; it does not automatically finalize physical columns, endpoints or operational parameters. A technical detail may remain explicitly deferred within a resolved decision.

### 1. Session / OTP mechanism — RESOLVED

The [auth/RBAC architecture](../security/AUTH_RBAC_ARCHITECTURE.md), opening and final paragraphs, requires verified actors, bounded OTP and expiry/revocation; the [vertical-slice roadmap](../ROADMAP_PHASE_2.md), Slice 2 row, requires OTP abuse/replay evidence. Their previously open mechanism wording is superseded by the approved human choice: **six-numeric-digit application-generated OTP stored only as a cryptographic hash**, 5-minute expiry, maximum five verification attempts, **60-second resend cooldown**, latest-active-only per normalized contact/channel, immediate invalidation on success and rejection of expired/used/locked codes; replaceable sender interface with SMTP email and Android-gateway SMS for the MVP demonstration; explicitly enabled deterministic development/test sender. A **15-minute signed account JWT carrying persisted Session `sid` is issued only after successful OTP verification of email/phone already associated with the controlled-provisioned account**. Every authenticated request checks the Session; normal logout revokes only the current one and clears the cookie, so other sessions remain valid. Successful guest-channel OTP verification issues only a separate **15-minute** signed proof scoped to normalized contact, purpose and applicable event/context. No external auth/OTP provider or extra factor is selected or required.

**Slice 2 constraint:** verification/persistence owns challenge, attempts, cooldown, lockout, consumption and replay; sender delivery never establishes identity. The account path binds the challenge to an existing account-associated channel, checks the submitted OTP and persisted controls, and **only then** creates a Session and signs the JWT; contact submission, account existence, generation and delivery attempt are individually insufficient. Guest proof remains channel-scoped without account privileges. Server-side proof validation yields a verified account actor or bounded guest ownership context; central authorization resolves current scope independently. The minimum boundary must issue/verify challenges, establish/inspect account and guest proofs, persist/verify/revoke the current Session, clear the account cookie on logout, report bounded invalid/expired/replayed/throttled outcomes and send through a replaceable interface. The account JWT carries only subject/user ID, unique Session ID (`sid`), issuer, audience, issued-at, expiration and token ID if independently needed; signing material remains server-side. Use an HttpOnly cookie with production `Secure`/appropriate `SameSite`, never browser-readable storage, and CSRF protection for cookie-authenticated writes. Tests must exercise the real hash/expiry/attempt/replay path using synthetic identities and an explicitly enabled contained sender, not an always-successful stub or public bypass. The next-request revocation guarantee is enforced by checking the persisted Session on every authenticated request; logout revokes only the current Session and clears its cookie. Do not store raw JWTs, add a JWT blacklist or implement logout-all in Slice 2.

### 2. Identity linkage — RESOLVED

The [database architecture](../architecture/DATABASE_ARCHITECTURE.md), “Relational ownership and lifecycle” (`User / verified account` and `GuestIdentity / verification ownership` rows), and the [auth/RBAC architecture](../security/AUTH_RBAC_ARCHITECTURE.md), opening authentication paragraph, establish the conceptual distinction: an authenticated account has a verified identity and stable internal `User` linkage; a guest has a short-lived proof of ownership of a **particular** verified email or phone, without a required permanent account. [OPD-03 and OPD-05](../requirements/OPEN_PRODUCT_DECISIONS.md) preserve later registration ownership/duplicate rules without merging guests into accounts. The [role matrix](../requirements/ROLE_PERMISSION_MATRIX.md), “Rules that apply to every cell,” limits both to own records after proof.

**Implementation constraint:** controlled provisioning establishes the internal `User` and typed verified email and/or phone; normalize email by trimming/lowercasing and phone to E.164-style, and enforce uniqueness on normalized verified values so textual variants are not new identities. Keep the signed account JWT/internal `User` linkage separate from the 15-minute signed guest proof for the specific normalized OTP-verified channel/purpose/context; do not infer that an equal contact string is itself an account session or grant a guest staff rights. A `GuestIdentity` persistence row is justified only if durable linkage is actually needed; Slice 2 must not create an unverified permanent guest account or a generalized identity framework. Protected lookup, channel change and retention still need physical/privacy review, not permission to relax the verified-email **or** phone rule in Slice 4.

### 3. Organizer eligibility — RESOLVED

The [role matrix](../requirements/ROLE_PERMISSION_MATRIX.md), “Event: Create draft” and “Event Admin assignment” rows, [event lifecycle](../requirements/EVENT_LIFECYCLE.md), “Transition baseline,” and [OPD-01/OPD-17](../requirements/OPEN_PRODUCT_DECISIONS.md) establish event ownership and prevent Admin self-promotion/transfer. The approved human choice now fills the initial-authority gap: **no public self-service Organizer signup in MVP**; a verified account obtains a **platform-level Organizer-capability flag through controlled seed/administrative bootstrap**. A capable user may create events and becomes Organizer/owner **only of events they create**. Organizer request/approval is post-MVP.

**Slice 2 constraint:** controlled seed/administrative provisioning creates verified accounts and grants the platform capability only when explicitly approved; there is no public account signup, Organizer signup, self-grant API or participant-registration workflow here. Persist/protect the capability and controlled grant/removal audit without creating a public onboarding workflow or Event creation API. Test both capable and incapable verified accounts and ensure the flag alone grants no ownership of another event. Slice 3 owns real event creation and owner binding. The operational bootstrap command/actor remains subject to security review; do not turn test fixtures into production entitlement.

### 4. Staff grants / assignments — RESOLVED

The [role matrix](../requirements/ROLE_PERMISSION_MATRIX.md), “Event Admin assignment,” “Gate/volunteer assignment,” “Volunteer task,” and “Rules that apply to every cell,” plus the [auth/RBAC architecture](../security/AUTH_RBAC_ARCHITECTURE.md), actor table and enforcement paragraphs, lock the foundation: Organizer alone grants/revokes Event Admin; Organizer or an assigned Event Admin may assign Gate/Security or Volunteer within their event scope; Gate/Security is restricted to the assigned event **and gate**; Volunteer sees/updates only an own bounded task once that task exists. The [database architecture](../architecture/DATABASE_ARCHITECTURE.md), assignment/Gate/Volunteer rows, requires current scoped assignment facts and audited grant/revoke. No grant transfers event ownership or lets Admin promote another Admin.

**Implementation constraint:** persist independent event-scoped role/gate assignments; multiple legitimate grants can coexist, identical effective duplicates are blocked by a database constraint, and revoking one does not revoke the others. A Volunteer role assignment is not a volunteer task, general staff-management system or permission to see alerts/admin data. Use current, server-read assignment on each protected request and invalidate removed access. Organizer alone grants Event Admin; Organizer/assigned Admin may assign Gate/Security or Volunteer within the locked matrix, and no user may self-elevate. Fixture-backed events/gates suffice for Slice 2 policy tests; user-facing assignment screens and volunteer task/status flow wait for their roadmap owners. Re-grant representation and exact HTTP surface remain implementation contract choices; they may not override locked authority or cross-event isolation.

### 5. Audit fail-safe policy — RESOLVED

The [API contract](../api/API_CONTRACT.md), “Common command/read semantics,” requires protected facts and critical audit intent to commit atomically; the [database architecture](../architecture/DATABASE_ARCHITECTURE.md), “Transaction and constraint design to prove before schema,” previously deferred the fail-safe choice. The approved human decision now has three distinct categories:

1. **Critical state-changing operations:** grants/revocations, registration/cancellation, check-in/check-out, occupancy corrections and certificate issue/revoke fail closed if required audit cannot durably write. State change and audit record commit in **one database transaction**. Only grants/revocations and controlled Organizer-capability changes are Slice 2 state changes; the others belong to later slices.
2. **Security-control state:** OTP attempt counts, cooldowns and lockouts fail closed. If required state cannot persist, verification is rejected; successful token issuance must not bypass that write.
3. **Durable security audit versus diagnostic telemetry:** existing security/requirements documents retain durable append-oriented records for security-relevant events, expressly including privileged permission-denial attempts. If an authentication failure is security-relevant under [FR-AUD-001](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS.md), its required audit is durable too. Diagnostic logs/metrics for failures and denials may be best-effort duplicates, but their availability never satisfies a required audit obligation. On required audit-write failure, keep access denied and report a safe failure; do not claim the auditable event was completed. Neither audit nor telemetry contains OTP codes, tokens, passwords/secrets, unnecessary contact or sensitive credentials.

**Slice 2 constraint:** test critical audit-write failure, OTP security-control write failure, required denial-audit failure, and optional diagnostic-telemetry failure separately. Never treat best-effort telemetry as authorization to commit a critical change or to omit an audit event required by [FR-AUD-001](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS.md) and the [auth/RBAC architecture](../security/AUTH_RBAC_ARCHITECTURE.md). The audit/telemetry distinction is recorded in §11 without changing those sources.
